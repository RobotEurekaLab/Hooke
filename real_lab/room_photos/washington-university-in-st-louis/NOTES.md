# Washington University in St. Louis — Room Photo Research Notes

Facility per existing card: Molecular Microbiology Imaging Facility, Room 10302, McDonnell Pediatric Research Building.
Facility actually yielding room photos: **Washington University Center for Cellular Imaging (WUCCI)** — WashU's larger, better-documented cross-campus imaging core (noted in the existing card as an alternative candidate). No interior photos were found for the smaller Molecular Microbiology Imaging Facility itself, so this pass pivoted to WUCCI, which is clearly real, active, and photographically documented.

## What was found — downloaded photos
- `wucci_01_joens_geanon.jpg` (from https://outlook.washu.edu/app/uploads/2017/09/WUMS-0158.jpg): Two people in white lab coats and safety glasses at a bench. Foreground: a staff scientist adjusting a sample under a black-chassis instrument labeled "PLASMA ASHER" with a warning label; multiple flat-panel monitors behind showing a pseudocolored (magenta/green/gold) fluorescence micrograph and a grayscale reference image. Plain light-colored wall behind. Clearly a real working imaging-core bench, not a product shot.
- `wucci_02_oakley_roth.jpg` (from https://outlook.washu.edu/app/uploads/2017/09/WUMS-0153-0234.jpg): Two-panel composite. Top panel: a man in a lab coat looking into the binocular eyepieces of an upright fluorescence microscope (visible label "Axio Obs..." — a Zeiss Axio Observer-series scope) with green excitation light illuminating the stage, monitor beside it showing a fluorescence image; dim room typical of a fluorescence-imaging suite. Bottom panel: a woman in a lab coat (name badge partially reading "...Center for Cell...") operating a sample-processing/embedding instrument with a illuminated purple-lit chamber window; open shelving with containers and lab clutter visible directly behind her — genuine bench/shelving context, not a staged product photo.

Both images show real bench-level room context (walls, shelving, multiple pieces of equipment, people actively working), satisfying the room-level (not just single-equipment) bar. Two other candidate images from the same article (a close-up of a technician's hands inside an open cryo-EM instrument, and a person using a VR headset in a dim unidentifiable room) were reviewed and discarded — too tightly cropped / equipment-only to add room-level value.
